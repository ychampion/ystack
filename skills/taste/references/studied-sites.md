# The studied sites

Three public sites, studied from screen recordings in November 2026. They are references for
principles, not looks to copy: take what each one *decided*, never its colours, faces or layout.

## Scale AI (scale.com)

- **Imagery is the business, shown literally.** The hero is real footage from high-stakes settings (a
  surgeon, then ultrasound with its readouts, `24 fps`, `Qscan`), cutting every two seconds or so. Later
  an MRI scan carries annotation keypoints and a robotics photo is traced in white contour lines: data
  labelling made visible. None of it is stock decoration.
- **Scroll-driven 3D:** translucent planes (a photo, a contour layer, a data table) drift apart and
  rotate as you scroll, joined by dotted leader lines; headline words resolve with the scroll.
- **Type:** one wide geometric grotesk, regular weight, tight tracking; hero about 64 px, a full-width
  restatement of the promise at about 110 px in the footer. Small tracked mono uppercase labels name
  real categories (`APPLICATIONS`, `PUBLIC SECTOR`), each beside a tiny glyph.
- **Colour:** white, then full black for the applications story, then one deep green panel; photos and
  logos supply the rest. Cards are soft grey tone steps with no shadows.
- **Proof is real and named:** Mayo Clinic, Meta, the CDAO, each with a one-line outcome; co-branded
  tiles.
- **Copy:** high-stakes and plain. "The world's most important decisions need reliable AI systems."
- The hero headline is centred: once, over real footage, which is the one centred moment it earns.

## Maritime (maritime.sh)

- **One technique everywhere: halftone dithering.** A dithered sea and coast in grey on near-black; every
  product icon a dithered isometric cube (one box, two boxes, a swarm); a dithered globe, clock and
  people in the feature strip. The look comes from the name and is done in code.
- **Real product fragments:** a Windows-98-style accounting form for computer-use agents; a phone
  "Loadboard" with plausible loads (`#8812 Dallas → Tulsa $1,450`).
- **Specific claims:** "$1 / month per 2 GB", "From $0.011 / hour awake", "Wakes in 0.6 s",
  "Invite-only for now". Copy short and plain: "Every computer your agent needs."
- **Structure:** a 1 px hairline grid of comparable cells (three columns, then six), no shadows. The nav
  becomes a floating pill only once you scroll. A marquee of real employers' logos.
- **Agent-first action:** "Copy instructions for your agent" beside "Go to Dashboard"; a Frameworks
  column in the footer.

## boat (boat.dev)

- **The metaphor carries data.** A pixel-art night sea in one cobalt. "50 VMs at once · one boat = 1 VM
  of 2 vCPU" draws fifty boats; move the slider and the fleet changes. The picture is the chart.
- **Interactive proof:** a cost calculator (sessions per month, session length, peak VMs, CPU busy, VM
  size) against twelve named competitors, each with $/mo, a multiple and footnotes ("¹ disk resets when
  it sleeps"). The most persuasive section of the three sites.
- **Mono where it is true:** captions are literal commands; the comparison table is data; the install
  line has macOS/Linux/Windows tabs and a copy button; a "Step 1 of 3" demo types itself.
- **Small details:** the empty state "Nothing is running yet: nothing billed"; a footer that ends in a
  tmux status bar with real values; a crosshair cursor over the scene; the pixel sea dissolves into
  white instead of ending on a hard edge.
- **Type and colour:** one grotesk for everything but commands; cobalt, white and pale lavender tone
  steps: one hue, many values.

## What the three share

1. A concept taken from the subject: footage for decisions, dither for "maritime", boats for VMs.
2. One visual technique, used consistently.
3. Specific, checkable claims: prices, latencies, named customers, competitor numbers with footnotes.
4. The product shown working, not described.
5. One sans; a second face (mono) only where it means code or data.
6. Restrained colour: one ground, one ink, one accent or one hue family.
7. Motion tied to the story (scroll scrubs, a typing demo, a fleet that resizes), not fade-up everywhere.
